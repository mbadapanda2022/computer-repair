{# Series bulb (dim bulb) limiter for powering a repaired board #}

Series Bulb Limiter

The bench trick that saves a repaired SMPS or laptop board: an incandescent bulb in series with the live line. Its own hot resistance is the current limit, so a shorted board lights the bulb instead of blowing the fuse, the bridge or the track you just soldered.

Pick a bulb about the same wattage as the supply you are testing. Full brightness means a short somewhere — start with the bulk capacitor and the bridge; a bulb that flickers on then fades to dim is a healthy inrush charging the mains cap. A CFL or LED lamp will not work here, it needs a plain tungsten bulb.
A switched-mode supply is not a resistor, so the healthy-load figures are an estimate for sizing the bulb, not a promise about the voltage the board receives. Everything here sits on the live side of the mains: use an isolation transformer before probing anything with a meter or scope.
